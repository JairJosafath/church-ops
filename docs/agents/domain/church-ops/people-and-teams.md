# People and teams

A church runs on volunteers, and the app knows who they are, when they can
serve, who their children are and, sometimes, what they are going through.
In the Netherlands every one of those records is special category data
under the GDPR, because being known to a church says something about a
person's faith. This topic is about scheduling people without wearing them
out, and holding as little about them as the work needs, with the most
care for children and for what was told in confidence.

## Practices

- A person is one record whatever their role: a member, a guest, a
  volunteer, a child. Membership is a status with a start and an end, and
  serving in a team is a separate fact. A household links people who live
  together; a guardian link says who consents for a minor and who is
  contacted for them, and does not follow from the household.
- The lawful basis is written down per purpose. A church may process its
  members', former members' and regular contacts' data as a religious body
  for its own aims (GDPR article 9(2)(d)), and may not disclose it outside
  without consent. Anything beyond that (photos on the website, a name on
  the stream, a birthday in the newsletter) is consent, asked separately,
  recorded with a time, and withdrawable in one step.
- Minors' data is held through their guardians: a guardian gives the
  consents, sees what is held, and receives the messages. Under sixteen, a
  child's consent alone counts for nothing in the Netherlands (UAVG article
  5); from sixteen the young person consents for themselves, and the app
  asks them again when they turn sixteen.
- Volunteers who work with minors hold a current VOG, and the app records
  that it was seen and when it expires, not the certificate itself.
  Children's teams are scheduled with at least two adults per group (the
  four-eyes principle), and the schedule warns when a group has one.
- Check-in for children's work records who brought a child and who may
  collect them; the collection code is per visit, and the child's allergy
  or medical note is shown to that group's leaders for that session only.
- Pastoral data lives apart from the person record, with its own access
  list: the pastors and the care team the person would expect, not every
  admin. Every read is logged, a note says who wrote it, and a person may
  ask to see what is held about them, as with any other data. A prayer
  request is private unless its author marks it shareable, and then only
  with the circle they choose.
- Scheduling is a request, not an assignment: the scheduler proposes, the
  person accepts or declines, and only an accepted request appears on the
  run sheet. Requests respect blockouts, existing requests on the same day
  (one person cannot be on drums and on camera at the same time), and a
  per-person preference for how often they serve.
- Blockouts and availability are the person's own: they set them, they see
  them, and the scheduler sees only "unavailable", never the reason.
- Retention per purpose, each a setting with a default and a job that
  enforces it: a guest's details a short while after their last visit, a
  former member's after a few years unless they ask to stay in touch,
  check-in records per session for weeks, pastoral notes as long as the
  pastoral relationship lasts and then reviewed.
- The person record holds what the church uses: a name, a way to reach
  them, the teams, the household. The national identification number (BSN),
  a copy of an ID document, or a full date of birth when the year is
  enough, are not collected.
- Every party the data reaches (the e-mail sender, the chat adapters, the
  payment provider, a denominational membership registry) is named in the
  privacy notice and has a processing agreement.

## Common mistakes

- A member directory that every logged-in person can browse, with
  addresses, phone numbers and the children's names.
- A care note or a prayer request in a field of the person record that
  every admin, and every export, can see.
- Treating a child's own "yes" as consent to put their photo or name on the
  stream.
- Scheduling a volunteer into a blocked-out week because the scheduler
  overrode it without the person knowing.
- Putting the blockout's reason ("chemo", "divorce hearing") where the
  scheduler can read it.
- Scheduling the same person every week because they always say yes, until
  they stop coming.
- A children's group with one adult on the schedule and no warning.
- Keeping every guest card ever filled in, forever.
- A spreadsheet export of the whole roll e-mailed to a team leader.
- Deleting a person by deleting their history, so last year's schedule and
  giving records break, instead of anonymizing the person and keeping the
  facts.

## Controversies

- **One person record, or separate lists per ministry?** Ministries like
  owning their list. *The choice:* one person record, with the teams,
  households and statuses as facts about it, and access to each fact by
  role. Why: separate lists drift, and the same parent ends up with three
  addresses and no idea who holds which.
- **Consent or legitimate interest for members?** *The choice:* the
  religious body's own basis (article 9(2)(d)) for what the church needs to
  be a church with its members and regular contacts, and explicit consent
  for anything that leaves that circle or shows a person in public. Why: the
  exception fits the church's ordinary work, and consent is only freely
  given when saying no costs nothing.
- **May the scheduler override a blockout?** Sometimes there is nobody else.
  *The choice:* no; the scheduler may send a request that names the
  blockout, and the person decides. Why: a blockout is the person's word,
  and a volunteer who learns it does not count stops volunteering.
- **Auto-schedule or hand-schedule?** An algorithm fills a rota in seconds
  and does not know who is tired. *The choice:* the app proposes a schedule
  from availability, blockouts and frequency preferences, a person reviews
  and sends it, and every slot is still a request. Why: the proposal saves
  the scheduler's evening; the review keeps the human judgement.
- **Where do pastoral notes live?** In the app, or nowhere? *The choice:* in
  the app, in a separate store with its own access list and an access log,
  and only when the pastoral team asks for it; off by default. Why: notes
  that live in pastors' own phones and notebooks are not safer, only
  unaccounted for; but a church that does not need them should hold none.
